Laser Focus · AI Game Designer Assignment · Part 1

Guiding the player from "join a team" to "understand what a team is for"

Four onboarding beats — Context, Action, Consequence, Independence — implemented as contextual interventions across the existing tutorial-to-hub hand-off, plus one existing mid-match reward screen. Each intervention reuses the game's own coach-dialogue pattern established in the base tutorial.

Tutorial→ Profile / Flag→ ① Context — Choose Team→ ② Action — Team Hub→ Play Match→ ③ Consequence — Level Up → Rewards→ ④ Independence — Hand-off

Panel 1 · Choose Your Team

Provides context before team selection

Currently the player lands on this screen straight from profile setup, with no framing — just follower counts, divisions, and positions to compare.

This panel fires once, before the list becomes interactive. Dismissing it reveals the existing team list, unchanged.

Design rationale: reuses the tutorial's own coach-panel pattern — portrait, blue quote, plain body, single CTA — so it reads as a continuation of onboarding rather than a new system.

Panel 2 · Team Hub

Establishes visual hierarchy on a crowded hub

Ad Mega Box, Season Pass, and three separate league fixtures currently compete for attention with no signal of what matters first. One fixture on screen is already a completed 0:7 loss with no Play button.

Implementation rule: highlight the first fixture card with an active Play CTA, not the literal first card on screen, which may be a past or unplayable result.

Design rationale: nothing is removed or hidden. The Ad Mega Box and Season Pass rails stay visible, only de-emphasized under the highlight for this first visit.
Beat 3 · Consequence — Level Up

Leverages an existing reward moment

This toast fires automatically mid-match — unchanged from current game behavior. No new system, no interruption of pacing.

The player reaches this milestone during their first recommended team match from Beat 2, so the level-up now lands inside a team context rather than a generic solo session.

Design rationale: the tighter the gap between action and consequence, the stronger the moment reads. Nothing here is new — it's existing behavior, reached via the guided path.

Beat 3 · Consequence — Pointing at what already exists

Makes an existing progression entry point discoverable

Appears at the next natural break after the level-up toast — between innings, not mid-action — so match pacing isn't interrupted.

The highlighted indicator in the top-left is existing UI, live in the game today. This panel's role is discovery, not new functionality.

Design rationale: the lowest-cost intervention available — one highlight ring and one line of coach dialogue, pointing at something that was already there.

Beat 3 · Consequence — Level Rewards + Premium

Introduces Premium at a contextually relevant moment

Tapping the level indicator opens this existing rewards screen — the Free track the player just earned from, positioned directly beside the Premium tiers already built into the game.

A short system-voice nudge sits on top, matching the plain style used elsewhere for reward/XP callouts — no portrait, so it doesn't borrow Bertee's credibility for a purchase prompt. Both buttons carry equal visual weight.

Design rationale: the player has just seen a concrete reward from playing, in context, alongside what Premium adds on top of it — rather than being asked to spend before experiencing any payoff.

Either choice above leads straight back here — no detour, no extra screen.

Beat 3 · Consequence — Return to Match

Preserves the player's place in the match

Dismissing the rewards screen — Upgrade or Maybe Later — returns the player directly to their in-progress match. No hub detour, no additional navigation.

Design rationale: Beat 3 is a brief detour, not a destination. Preserving the player's match position keeps this a glance at a reward rather than a system they've been pulled away to learn.

Beat 4 · Independence — Hand-off

Delivers an explicit hand-off

Lands back on the hub with all systems visible and un-highlighted — nothing left de-emphasized. This is where guidance formally ends.

Bookends Panel 1 deliberately: same character, same tone, but "you can't do this alone" flips into "you've got this now" — a clear signal that onboarding is complete.

Design rationale: an explicit close avoids the ambiguity of guidance simply stopping. The player is told, not left to infer, that they're on their own from here.
Mockups are built on real screenshots from first-session playthroughs — primarily Homerun, with one screen (the Level Rewards detail in Beat 3) sourced from Goal, where the same mechanic shows clearer Free/Premium contrast in-frame. Visual language reuses the game's existing tutorial pattern; no new UI pattern is introduced.
